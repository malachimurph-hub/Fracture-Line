# M3.4 (in progress, not released)

From Malachi's play-test of 0.3.5.

## Night lights
- Vehicle headlights now follow the drawn (interpolated) vehicle every frame instead of 8 times a
  second, so they no longer stutter when vehicles move.
- Each beam comes from one of the model's own headlights. The lamp positions are read from the
  model geometry (warm LAMP-class vertices at the front of the hull), with one beam per lamp and a
  lens flare on each. The beam meets the ground just past the lower hull (a dozer blade or bumper,
  not the gun barrel). The 0.3.5 direction fix (hull forward, tilt-aware) is kept.
- Structures no longer sit in a glowing apron. They show only their own lamps and lit windows,
  plus 2 to 4 floodlights on their walls and roof corners: a lens flare, a faint shaft, and an
  oval pool where each one points. Colours are per faction: US cool LED, Germany neutral, Iran
  sodium, Russia warm. Walls and other 1-tile pieces have none, and civilian blocks keep only
  their windows.
- Still phone-light: no real lights, only additive MultiMesh quads (one draw call per group),
  capped at 240 floodlights, nearest first.

## Infantry
- All soldiers are new, original procedural models (tools/gfx_m34; no third-party assets). The
  former toon-kit base is gone from the game.
  - Adult proportions (about 7.6 heads tall).
  - Lofted, smooth-shaded body.
  - Gear per faction: helmets with cover, rails, NVG mount, goggles and chin strap; plate
    carriers or chest rigs with magazine, admin, radio and IFAK pouches; belts; knee pads;
    gloves; boots.
  - Packs per role: assault pack, hydration, radio with whip antenna, rocket pack, spare tube,
    engineer tools, ghillie, MG ammo.
- Faction looks:
  - US: coyote blotch camo.
  - Germany: spot camo.
  - Iran-style militia: shemagh, caps, wraps and beards.
  - Russia: fine green camo, Russian-style helmet.
  - Infiltrator and saboteur: black, with balaclavas.
- Camouflage is smooth noise anchored to the cloth: no grain, no tiling, and it does not swim
  during animation.
- Variety:
  - Four look variants per unit type: head gear and packs.
  - Per-soldier skin tone, uniform tint and height (+-4 %), with clip timing offsets.
- Weapons are held in both hands in every clip. The right hand is on the grip and the left on the
  handguard, front grip or tube.
- Aimed fire uses a bladed stance with the cheek on the stock and recoil. Launchers are fired
  from the shoulder, and pistols with both hands.
- New clips:
  - Breathing idle with a look-around, a walk, and a tactical run with the rifle at port arms.
  - Kneeling fire: marksmen always kneel, and some riflemen do.
  - Barricade lean.
  - Two deaths: falling backwards, and crumpling forwards. The weapon drops to the ground.
- New procedural weapons: combat shotgun (Breacher), compact PDW (Sturmtrupp), pistol
  (Insurgent and Saboteur), and an entrenching shovel (Labourer).
- Compact VAT format:
  - Position packed 11/11/10 bits in RGBA8, octahedral normals in RG8, 10 fps with frame
    blending.
  - About 2,900 triangles per soldier, with LODs.
  - All 21 bakes total about 15 MB, the same as before.

## Title: "Fracture Line: Modern Conflict" and the official emblem

- The subtitle is now **Modern Conflict** (was Global Conflict): window title, menu logo, boot
  splash, credits, error screen, strings, README, Play listing and privacy policy drafts. The
  launcher label stays "Fracture Line"; the package stays com.malachimurphy.fractureline.
- Malachi's official emblem (`assets_src/emblem/emblem_user.png`) is now the menu logo, the boot
  splash, the launcher icon (adaptive: the shield in the 66 dp safe zone over a blurred copy of the
  emblem's red/blue battle background, plus a themed monochrome layer; legacy 192 px), the project
  icon and the Play 512 px icon and feature graphic. The shield is cut out with a feathered
  hexagon, keeping its red/blue glow. All generated by `tools/gen_emblem_m34.py`.
- The old procedural badge, splash, tank icons and their generator are kept in `ui/logo/superseded`,
  `assets/icons/superseded`, `tools/superseded` and `builds/play/superseded` (not packaged).

## Structure shading (Malachi: "the shading on the structures might be a little too dark")

- Without the old glow round buildings, structures and hulls read as near-black silhouettes by
  moonlight: the colour ambient barely lifts their (dark, linear) paint and metal reflects only
  the black background. Models now get a cheap sky fill (`fl_fill` shader global, set per map by
  `MapEnv.model_fill`): albedo x fill colour, brighter facing up, a little on walls, none from
  below, and metal eased toward diffuse. Moonlit maps use a strong cool fill; day maps a little
  of the ambient so shadowed walls keep their detail. Civilian buildings and other solid props
  get the same fill. No lights are added (floodlights and lamps are still the only light
  sources), no glow, and the infantry shader is unchanged. A few ALU per pixel.

## Minerals Mining pack-up animation (Malachi: "Pack up animation for Russian minerals mining")

- The Russian Minerals Mining bucket-wheel excavator already took 10 s (sim ticks) to pack up and
  10 s to set up again, but it just stood still and then turned into the vehicle. It now
  animates in step with those timers (no sim timing change, saves and replays unaffected):
  the bucket wheel spins down, the mast stays are unhooked, the discharge conveyor swings in
  along the house, the bucket-wheel boom lowers and draws in, the A-frame mast folds back over the
  counterweight, the spoil heaps are levelled and four new stabiliser jacks lift and retract
  under the turntable. The vehicle drives in the packed pose and Set Up plays the sequence in
  reverse. A cancelled pack-up eases back out.
- Model split into separately posed parts (`tools/gfx_m33/new_m33.gd`, re-baked in its old atlas
  slot); `view/buildings/pack_rig.gd` poses them (a node transform each, no extra draw calls).

## Income popups (Malachi: "For all passive income buildings, have the amount it generates pop up and fade like supply trucks do")

- Every passive income structure you own (Data Center, EU Hub, Automotive Plant, Oil Derrick,
  captured pumps; the Russian mine already popped its $400 lump sum) now shows a "+$N" popup
  with the supply trucks' floater (same colour, rise and fade) every 10 s: what that structure
  earned in those 10 s (it pays every second, which would be too noisy). Staggered per
  structure. Worked out on the view side from the same rules as the economy (up to four copies
  pay, low power halves, Logistics doubles); exactly what the sim paid, no sim change. Only your
  own structures, only when on view; spectators see none.
- Floaters (trucks too) get a thin dark outline so they read on bright ground; at most 40 alive
  (drawn text, no nodes).

## United States music (Malachi's own tracks)

- Malachi's four USA tracks from his Drive "USA MUSIC" folder, converted to OGG Vorbis 128 kbps
  (10.8 MB total): **Base Command** is now the main-menu music (loops) and opens every US match;
  the US in-match shuffle is Base Command, Defend the Border, Mission Accomplished and Zero Hour

  longer packaged. Russia, Germany and Iran music unchanged. Credits updated.

## Live battle behind the main menu

- The main menu plays a live battle again (`ui/menu_battle.gd`, a rewrite of the M2.6 to M3.0.1
  backdrop that was removed in 0.3.1): big vehicle battles only (tanks, IFVs, self-propelled
  guns, helicopters and jets flying from each side's airfield, no infantry) between two random
  factions in front of their bases, with explosions, smoke and burning wrecks. Four slow
  high, wide camera moves (pan along the front, orbit, crane over a base, diagonal fly-over),
  each about 16 s; three per map, then it fades to the key art and moves to the next map,
  alternating day and night (night: headlights, floodlights, street lamps, muzzle flashes).
- Phone budget: at most 16 units a side, lighter effects, no shadows, 67% render scale, the
  menu capped at 30 fps, the sim at most 2 steps a frame (it slows down rather than bursting).
  If the frame rate stays under 22 fps for 4 s it fades out and the static key art stays for the
  rest of the session; the Low preset and the compatibility renderer always use the key art.
- A subtle dark vignette behind the buttons keeps them and the emblem readable.
